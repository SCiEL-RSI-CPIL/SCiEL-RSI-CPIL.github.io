<Sec id="idea" />

A system improves *recursively* when its improvements <span class="key">make it better at improving</span> <a class="cite" href="#ref-14">(Yampolskiy, 2015)</a>. People do this through culture: what one person learns is taught, tried by others, and kept if it works <a class="cite" href="#ref-15">(Henrich, 2016)</a>. SCiEL applies the same loop to a population of programs written by a language model:

- **Demes.** The population is split into demes, groups of programs that evolve on their own, in parallel.
- **Findings travel after a test.** A deme turns a hunch into an experiment. What holds becomes a *finding*, which is delivered to other demes and kept only if it helps there.
- **A policy and a world model steer.** The policy learns which kinds of edit pay off; the world model predicts whether an edit will pay off and skips likely failures before they cost an evaluation.
- **Every choice is randomised and logged**, so SCiEL can measure from its own records whether a change to itself helped.

We run SCiEL in the [Empira arena](https://research-arena.small-pine-3af7.workers.dev/experiments), an independent benchmark of research tasks, with an open 27B model served locally.

<Sec id="why" />

**A single model, a single climber or a crowd that does not share all stall; SCiEL keeps climbing.** A hard problem is a long climb seen one edit at a time. Sampling one model many times never builds on a success. One climber builds, but stops at the first bump. Several climbers that share nothing split the budget and each stop at their own bump. On a toy landscape with a trap and a narrow ridge to the summit, SCiEL reaches the top in 29 of 30 trials. Removing the learned policy, the world model or culture costs the most:

<Sec id="top" />

**Near the best program, most edits stop helping, and that is where a run's budget goes.** Across all edits, 30% to 80% beat their parent. Starting from a program close to the run's best, the share drops in five of the six runs, to as low as 7%. This is where shared lessons and long shots matter most, and the regime SCiEL is built for.

<Sec id="use" />

**SCiEL fits a task when three things are true:**

1. **You can check an answer you trust**, exactly or on data the search never saw.
2. **The best known answer can still be beaten** by more than the noise in your score.
3. **Progress hides behind rare long shots**: most tries fail, and the ones that matter are bold.

Not sure? `sciel task ./materials` drafts a task from your papers, notebooks or data, and checks all three before any compute is spent.

<Sec id="next" />

- **Across generations.** We will run the same tasks generation after generation, each starting from what the last one learned, against fresh runs at equal cost. If culture compounds, later generations should improve faster.
- **Into the weights.** With DeltaServe <a class="cite" href="#ref-13">[13]</a>, small adapters (LoRA) can be trained on the run's own verified improvements while it searches. An adapter is kept only if it passes the same test as any other finding.
- **More tasks.** The mechanism is not specific to mathematics; the Empira arena has 959 task packages to try it on.
