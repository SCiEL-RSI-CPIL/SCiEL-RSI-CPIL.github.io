<Sec id="idea" />

A system improves *recursively* when its improvements <span class="key">make it better at improving</span> (<a class="cite" href="#ref-14">Yampolskiy, 2015</a>). People do this through culture: what one person learns is taught, tried by others, and kept if it works (<a class="cite" href="#ref-15">Henrich, 2016</a>). SCiEL applies the same loop to programs written by a language model. Its population is split into *demes*, groups of programs that evolve on their own, in parallel. Each deme repeats one step; between demes, what one deme learns travels as a tested *finding*:

<Sec id="results" />

**SCiEL reaches or beats the best known values on two open mathematical problems, at a cost of a few dollars per run.** All runs are in the [Empira arena](https://research-arena.small-pine-3af7.workers.dev/experiments), an independent benchmark of research tasks, with an open 27B model served locally.

| Task | Result | Compared with |
|---|---|---|
| Circle packing, n = 26 | **best known value**, zero tolerance | best known |
| Third autocorrelation constant | **tighter bound** | AlphaEvolve (<a class="cite" href="#ref-3">Novikov et al., 2025</a>) |
| Same model and budget | **higher scores, ~½ the evaluations** | OpenEvolve, ShinkaEvolve (<a class="cite" href="#ref-1">Sharma, 2025</a>; <a class="cite" href="#ref-2">Lange et al., 2025</a>) |
| Circle packing on two GPUs | **six digits** of the best known value | a 4.4× larger model (<a class="cite" href="#ref-4">Yuksekgonul et al., 2026</a>) |

<Sec id="why" />

**A single model, a single climber or a crowd that does not share all stall; SCiEL keeps climbing.** To see why SCiEL needs every part of Figure 2, we use a toy landscape small enough to search thousands of times. A hard problem is a long climb seen one edit at a time. Sampling one model many times never builds on a success. One climber builds, but stops at the first bump. Several climbers that share nothing split the budget and each stop at their own bump. The toy has a trap and a narrow ridge to the summit, and the difference is visible in a single trial:

<Climb />

Over 30 trials, SCiEL reaches the summit in 29. Switching off one part of Figure 2 at a time shows what each is worth: without the learned policy, the world model or culture, it gets there in 8 to 15. Research cells add little here, because on this landscape the slope always leads somewhere.

<Sec id="culture" />

**Yes: receiving a finding from another deme raises the chance that an edit improves from 54% to 62%.** The toy above suggests culture matters; real runs can test it directly. When a deme writes its next prompt, SCiEL flips a weighted coin to decide whether to include a finding from another deme, and logs the odds. That turns every run into a randomised experiment. <Sec id="top" />

**Near the best program, most edits stop helping, and that is where a run's budget goes.** Across all edits, 30% to 80% beat their parent. Starting from a program close to the run's best, the share drops in five of the six runs, to as low as 7%. This is where shared lessons and long shots matter most, and the regime SCiEL is built for.
